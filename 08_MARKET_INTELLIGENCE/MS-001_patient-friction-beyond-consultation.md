## 1. Source content captured

The post's central observation:
> Patients often walk away over the admin before the care even comes into it.

In a reply, Max Mamoyco stated that, in a selected sample of negative reviews:
- 51.9% involved billing and pricing.
- 29% involved prescription or refill problems.
- 16.8% were coded for rushed or generic consultations.
- Categories overlap.

Other comments in the supplied excerpt raised these themes:
- Billing, follow-up and handoffs between visits can determine whether patients stay with a provider.
- Digital healthcare products should measure continuity, prescription management, billing clarity and patient support—not consultation completion alone.
- Clear pricing, visible refill status and simple cancellation may reduce avoidable friction.
- AI may help triage support and refill requests, with human handling of edge cases and no autonomous clinical decision-making.

Therefore, do not generalize these percentages to all patients, clinics, geographies or healthcare markets. Category overlap means the percentages must not be summed.

Comments are individual professional opinions and anecdotes, not proof of prevalence or causal effect.

## 3. Curalives implications — hypotheses, not approved scope

Potential workflow areas to investigate:
1. Transparent billing, estimates and payment status.
2. Prescription/refill request status and clear next steps.
3. Follow-up reminders and continuity after a visit.
4. Patient support triage and escalation to a human.
5. Clear cancellation/rescheduling and handoff status.
6. Measure completed care journeys and resolved administrative issues, not just completed consultations.

Potential AI boundary:
- AI may classify, summarize or route administrative requests.
- Human review should handle ambiguity, safety concerns and exceptions.
- No autonomous diagnosis, prescribing, medication changes or clinical triage decisions without a separately validated and approved clinical governance model.

## 4. Discovery questions

- Which administrative failure most often causes patients to abandon a clinic or switch providers?
- Which tasks consume the most staff time between visits?
- How are billing disputes, refill requests and follow-ups handled today?
- What proportion of requests are resolved on first contact?
- What status information do patients repeatedly ask staff to provide?
- Who pays for solving each problem: clinic owner, operations team, provider or patient?
- What evidence would demonstrate improved retention, reduced workload or better continuity?

## 5. Candidate measures to validate

- Time from patient request to resolution.
- First-contact resolution rate.
- Billing query / dispute rate.
- Refill request completion time and exception rate.
- Follow-up completion rate.
- Patient-reported clarity of price and next step.
- Staff time spent on administrative coordination.
- Patient retention / repeat-visit rate, with an explicit denominator and cohort definition.
