Technical Requirements These are the quality attributes and constraints Doctor OS Lite Phase 1 must meet, stated independently of the technology stack, which a CTO or technical architect will select. They are acceptance gates for pilot readiness, not aspirations: a build that fails offline reliability or approval integrity should not enter a clinic. Targets are sized for a pilot of a small number of clinics, not national scale, so the architecture should not be over-built for scale the venture has no evidence it will reach. Performance The patient context screen loads in under two seconds, offline or online. A clinical-note draft is generated in under thirty seconds after voice capture ends, when online. Patient search returns results in under one second for fuzzy name or phone matches. The app cold-starts in under three seconds on a low-end Android reference device. A billing receipt is produced in under two seconds from approval to an exportable document. Reliability and Offline Behavior All Phase 1 core workflows (patient context, voice capture, clinical-note approval, queue, and billing) function with zero network connectivity. Zero data loss on app crash during a consult; a local write persists before any processing begins. Clinical-data conflicts require explicit resolution and are never silently overwritten. Operational conflicts (such as queue and visit status) converge deterministically under the approved last-write-wins policy, and every accepted outcome is logged so neither change is silently lost from the audit trail. Every clinical note has a logged approval event (doctor identity, timestamp, device) before it enters the record. Security and Privacy Data is encrypted at rest from the first build, not retrofitted. Data is encrypted in transit for all network calls. Explicit, purpose-specific, revocable consent for voice capture and health-data use is captured and logged before a patient record is created. Only data fields required for the Phase 1 feature set are collected; no speculative data capture. The CTO and compliance owner must define the applicable breach-reporting obligations, reporting window, accountable role, reportable-event criteria, and validation method before production deployment. This is a pending compliance decision, not a Phase 1 pilot acceptance gate. Where multiple clinics are supported, there is zero cross-clinic data visibility, verified before any multi-clinic use. App access is protected by a device lock (biometric or PIN), with identity verification at onboarding. Every clinical data access or creation event is logged with actor, timestamp, and purpose. Usability and Accessibility A first-time doctor completes a first consult unaided within a few minutes of opening the app. A new front-desk user manages queue and billing competently within about fifteen minutes. Body text meets a minimum contrast ratio of 4.5:1. Interactive elements have a minimum touch target of 44 by 44 pixels. Text scales up to 200 percent without breaking the layout. Maintainability and Future-Proofing Data models are FHIR-compatible in structure from day one, even if not fully FHIR-compliant at pilot stage, to avoid a costly migration when ABDM integration is added later. The architecture allows later additions (live ABDM exchange, additional languages, NFC or hardware layers) without a full rewrite of the core data model. Scope Boundaries and Constraints Live ABDM integration is out of scope for Phase 1; the pilot designs for compatibility but does not depend on external record exchange. AI is used to draft clinical notes only; the final clinical record always requires doctor review and approval. AI does not prescribe. Billing records a receipt and payment status; there is no payment gateway in the pilot. The low-end Android phone is the primary reference device; targets assume this hardware floor. No production infrastructure is built before a CTO or technical architect is engaged; interim architecture work is for design and feasibility only. Non-Functional Requirements Specification — Doctor OS Lite (Phase 1) This specification consolidates the approved quality attributes for Doctor OS Lite Phase 1. The Technical Requirements overview remains authoritative for non-functional targets. The container and component blueprints remain authoritative for technical design, synchronization, security boundaries, and observability. This document provides a single reference without adding targets that the project has not approved. Performance The system must meet the approved pilot performance targets on the low-end Android reference device: Patient Context Screen loads in under two seconds, offline or online. Online clinical-note draft generation completes in under thirty seconds after voice capture ends. Fuzzy patient search by name or phone responds in under one second. App cold start completes in under three seconds. Billing receipt generation completes in under two seconds from request to exportable receipt. The Android Clinic App keeps work on the local working copy during a consultation. Background synchronization and drafting activity must not block patient context, voice capture, or billing interaction. Security All clinic data on the device is encrypted at rest from the first build. All network calls use encrypted transport. Named staff accounts authenticate during onboarding and then require biometric or PIN unlock on the registered device. Every request and local action is scoped to the staff member's clinic; cross-clinic data visibility is prohibited. The Clinic API is authoritative for reconciliation and enforces clinic scope at the data-access boundary. The Android client never directly calls a remote AI provider. Remote drafting passes through the Clinic API, which validates staff access and consent before any provider call. Availability The approved artifacts define no server uptime target or service-level agreement for the pilot. The approved availability strategy is functional continuity at the clinic: core journeys continue on the encrypted local working copy when the Clinic API or network is unavailable. The CTO must define service availability targets, maintenance behavior, dependency availability expectations, and escalation ownership before production deployment. Reliability The Phase 1 core workflows (patient context, voice capture, clinical-note approval, queue, and billing) must work with zero network connectivity. A local write persists before processing begins, so app crashes and power loss do not lose in-progress consult data. Synchronization uses a durable local journal, idempotent changes, version-aware reconciliation, and automatic retry after connectivity returns. Operational records, such as queue changes, resolve under last-write-wins rules. Clinical notes never auto-merge: a concurrent clinical conflict requires explicit clinician resolution. Every approved clinical note must carry doctor identity, time, and device in its approval event. Scalability The approved scale target is a pilot of a small number of clinics, with a minimum of ten clinics supported without architecture rework, up to 5,000 patient records per clinic without performance degradation, and up to three clinic contexts per doctor profile. These are directional pilot targets rather than national-scale projections. The architecture isolates data by clinic and uses a server-authoritative record with local working copies. This supports later growth in devices and clinics, but national-scale capacity, throughput, and concurrency targets are not approved and remain CTO decisions. Accessibility The product must support doctors and front-desk staff with low training tolerance and the age range captured by the approved personas. Body text requires a 4.5:1 minimum contrast ratio, interactive elements require a 44 by 44 pixel minimum touch target, and text must scale up to 200 percent without layout breakage. A first-time doctor must be able to complete a first consultation unaided within a few minutes. A front-desk staff member must be able to manage queue and billing competently within about fifteen minutes. Offline Behavior The Android Clinic App owns a complete encrypted local working copy of clinic data. Patient search, patient registration, context view, queue, voice capture, note review and approval, billing, and receipt generation operate against that local copy without a network. The app records local changes in a durable queue and synchronizes automatically in the background when connectivity is available. It retries failed synchronization with exponential backoff and preserves captured audio and note drafts until the doctor approves the note. When on-device drafting is unavailable offline, the app stores the capture locally and resumes remote drafting after connectivity returns. Manual note creation remains available if drafting fails. Privacy The system collects only Phase 1 data fields. It requires explicit, purpose-specific, revocable consent for health-data use before storing a Patient Record and for voice capture before recording starts. Consent records are attributed to the patient, actor, time, and device. Raw consultation audio is kept only until the doctor approves the corresponding note and is then deleted. The approved clinical note and its approval event remain in the record. Audit records capture the action and purpose, not raw audio or clinical-note content. All clinic and patient data is stored in India. Compliance The approved constraints are compliance-adjacent and do not claim formal certification. They include consent capture, data minimization, encryption, audit logging, clinic data isolation, and the capacity to detect and support breach reporting within the regulatory window. Clinical data models are FHIR-compatible in structure from the first build to avoid a costly later migration. Live ABDM integration and formal FHIR exchange are out of scope for Phase 1. AI only drafts documentation; it does not prescribe, approve, sign, or independently write into the clinical record. Maintainability The architecture separates the Android Clinic App, Clinic API, and shared components for synchronization, identity/consent/audit, and clinical-note drafting. Data models are FHIR-compatible, and the drafting interface supports on-device or remote implementations without binding features to a provider. API contracts are versioned so supported older app builds can continue to synchronize during rollout. Technology stack, hosting platform, database, AI provider, encryption mechanism, and deployment architecture are still CTO decisions. Auditability The system records patient-data access, patient-record creation, consent actions, recording start, note edits, approval, audio deletion, billing actions, and synchronization outcomes as append-only audit events. Each event includes the actor, time, device, clinic scope, action, entity reference, and purpose. Device events persist locally before later central storage, so outages do not erase the audit trail. Audit events are centrally stored once synchronization resumes. The approved artifacts do not define audit retention periods or reporting workflows; those are open policy and CTO decisions. Disaster Recovery The approved artifacts define crash recovery for the app and reconciliation after network interruption, but they do not define server backup, restoration targets, regional failover, recovery point objective, recovery time objective, or a tested disaster-recovery runbook. Before production deployment, the CTO must define and validate disaster recovery for the authoritative Clinic API data store and the audit store. That plan must respect India data residency, encryption, clinic isolation, and the local-device recovery behavior already specified. Monitoring The Clinic API must record non-clinical operational signals: synchronization success and conflict rates, drafting fallback rate and latency, authentication failures, and audit-backlog status. Monitoring must avoid logging clinical content. Alert thresholds, dashboards, log retention, incident response process, and monitoring ownership are not approved yet. Technical Overview Doctor OS Lite is a two-container system: an offline-first Android clinic app and a server-side clinic API, connected by an authenticated synchronization channel. The app holds a complete encrypted local working copy so all Phase 1 workflows run without connectivity; the API is authoritative for reconciliation and stores the canonical record in India. Three shared capabilities (offline synchronization and conflict resolution, identity/consent/audit, and clinical-note drafting) support four features (patient records and context, patient queue, voice capture and clinical notes, billing and receipts). Authoritative design: @Android Clinic App and @Clinic API. System Context The clinic operates two devices per clinic (doctor and front-desk), each running the @Android Clinic App and reconciling only through the @Clinic API; the devices never peer directly. The single external dependency during operation is the Clinic API, which also fronts the remote drafting provider. Live ABDM exchange and patient-facing systems are out of scope for Phase 1 (per PRD and Technical Requirements overview). Solution Architecture The approved architecture is server-authoritative with full local working copies. Reads and writes hit the local copy first; reconciliation is background and authoritative on the server. See @Android Clinic App (ADR-001) and @Clinic API (ADR-001) for the authoritative decisions. Container Architecture @Android Clinic App: native Android client for a low-end reference device; encrypted local working copy; named sessions; background sync; owns all clinic UI entry points. @Clinic API: server container holding the authoritative, India-resident record; owns sync, identity, consent, audit intake, and the drafting gateway; contracts are versioned. Concrete language, framework, hosting, datastore, and deployment topology are pending (CTO Decision Register). Component Architecture Shared capabilities, all authoritative in their blueprints: @Offline Synchronization and Conflict Resolution: durable local journal, sync client and service, connectivity monitor, typed conflict resolver. @Identity, Consent and Audit: named sessions, clinic-scoped identity, purpose-specific consent, local audit recorder and central audit service. @Clinical Note Drafting: drafting coordinator, on-device and remote adapters, server drafting gateway and provider adapter, draft review boundary. Module Responsibilities The four features compose the shared capabilities: @Patient Records and Context, @Patient Queue, @Voice Capture and Clinical Notes, and @Billing and Receipts. Each feature blueprint defines its feature-specific components and how it configures the shared capabilities. Work orders WO-11 through WO-18 realize the feature layer; WO-1 through WO-10 realize the containers and shared components. Data Flow A user action writes to the local working copy first (write-ahead), then background sync sends a SyncBatch of ChangeRecord entries to the @Clinic API, which reconciles and returns authoritative updates and any SyncConflict. Clinical content requires explicit conflict resolution; operational content uses last-write-wins. Remote drafting flows app to @Clinic API gateway to provider and back as DraftingRequest/DraftResult. Audio never leaves the device; only a drafting request does. Authoritative detail: @Offline Synchronization and Conflict Resolution and @Clinical Note Drafting. Synchronization Architecture Durable journal, idempotency keys, version-vector optimistic concurrency, exponential backoff with jitter, and typed conflict rules. Authoritative: @Offline Synchronization and Conflict Resolution. Verifiable behavior: Acceptance Criteria document, offline and synchronization sections. Offline Architecture All Phase 1 workflows run against the encrypted local working copy; write-ahead persistence guarantees zero data loss on crash; captures and drafts persist until approval; manual notes remain available if drafting fails. Authoritative: @Android Clinic App and @Voice Capture and Clinical Notes; targets in the Technical Requirements overview and NFR. Security Architecture Encryption at rest and in transit; named-account access with device unlock; clinic isolation enforced at the API data-access layer; remote AI access only through the @Clinic API; India data residency. Authoritative: Technical Requirements overview, @Clinic API, @Identity, Consent and Audit. Encryption mechanism and hosting security specifics are pending (CTO Decision Register). Identity and Access Management Named staff accounts verified at onboarding; offline biometric or PIN unlock on registered devices; clinic-scoped sessions; cross-clinic access prohibited. Roles are limited to doctor and front-desk in Phase 1; administrator and auditor roles are future scope. Authoritative: @Identity, Consent and Audit (ADR-001). Audit Architecture Append-only, idempotent audit events for access, creation, consent, approval, deletion, billing, and sync outcomes, buffered locally and stored centrally. Content is excluded from audit and operational logs. Authoritative: @Identity, Consent and Audit (ADR-003). Retention periods and reporting workflows are pending (CTO Decision Register). AI Integration Architecture Provider-neutral drafting with runtime on-device or remote selection; remote drafting isolated behind the @Clinic API gateway and provider adapter; AI drafts only and never prescribes or approves; audio deleted after approval. Authoritative: @Clinical Note Drafting (ADR-001, ADR-004). Provider selection and on-device engine are pending (CTO Decision Register). API Contracts Versioned contracts at the @Clinic API boundary: SyncBatch, DraftingRequest/DraftResult, ConsentRecord, and AuditEvent. Contracts are FHIR-compatible in structure. Authoritative shapes and semantics live in @Clinic API and the referenced component blueprints; concrete transport and schema encodings are an implementation choice within the versioned contract. Database Requirements Authoritative, clinic-isolated, encrypted, India-resident storage of Patient, QueueEntry, ClinicalNote, Bill, ConsentRecord, AuditEvent, and ChangeRecord, with FHIR-compatible structure and version metadata for optimistic concurrency. Authoritative model detail: the four feature blueprints and @Clinic API. Datastore engine and isolation mechanism are pending (CTO Decision Register). Performance Requirements Approved targets (Technical Requirements overview and NFR): context load under two seconds; online draft under thirty seconds; search under one second; cold start under three seconds; receipt under two seconds. Not restated here as new targets. Reliability Requirements Offline operation for all core workflows; zero data loss on crash; zero silent overwrites of clinical data; logged approval on every note. Authoritative: Technical Requirements overview, @Offline Synchronization and Conflict Resolution, @Voice Capture and Clinical Notes. Scalability Requirements Sized for a small clinic pilot: at least ten clinics without rework, up to 5,000 records per clinic, up to three clinic contexts per doctor; clinic-isolated data supports growth. National-scale capacity is not approved. Authoritative: Technical Requirements overview and NFR. Error Handling Classified retriable versus terminal sync errors; preserved capture and draft on drafting failure with retry and manual fallback; validation on registration, line items, and status transitions; explicit clinical-conflict resolution. Authoritative: the feature blueprints, @Offline Synchronization and Conflict Resolution, @Clinical Note Drafting; verifiable behavior in the Acceptance Criteria document. Logging and Monitoring Non-clinical operational signals only: sync success and conflict rates, drafting fallback rate and latency, authentication failures, and audit-backlog status. Authoritative observability notes: @Clinic API. Thresholds, alert routing, dashboards, and log retention are pending (CTO Decision Register and NFR open decisions). Every step works offline and reconciles when connectivity returns. Arrival and identification — Front-desk staff find the patient by name or phone, or register a new patient with minimum fields and recorded health-data consent. (Patient Records and Context) Queue — Staff add the patient to the current-day queue as waiting, and manage order and status through the day. (Patient Queue) Pre-consult context — The doctor opens the patient and sees a short history summary of prior notes and prescriptions before starting. (Patient Records and Context) Consent and capture — The doctor confirms voice-capture consent, then records the consultation; capture is preserved locally as it records. (Voice Capture and Clinical Notes; Identity, Consent and Audit) Drafting — A SOAP draft is prepared on-device where possible, or via the remote drafting boundary using a consented transcript; the doctor can always fall back to a manual note. (Clinical Note Drafting) Review, edit, prescription — The doctor edits every field and adds an optional prescription within the note. (Voice Capture and Clinical Notes) Approval — The doctor explicitly approves; only then does the note enter the record with a logged approval event, and the raw audio is deleted. (Voice Capture and Clinical Notes) Visit close in queue — The visit status advances to done and leaves the waiting view. (Patient Queue) Billing and receipt — Staff itemize charges, generate a shareable receipt, and record payment status; no money moves through the app. (Billing and Receipts) Accountability throughout — Every access, creation, consent action, approval, and billing event is logged, clinic-scoped, and encrypted; conflicts across the two devices resolve safely.
