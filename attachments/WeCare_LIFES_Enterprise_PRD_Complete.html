1. Executive Overview & Vision

Strategic Vision

WeCare LIFES is a two-sided ambulatory healthcare operating system for independent and small multi-doctor clinics in semi-urban and Tier-2/3 India, with a connected patient and caregiver product that turns doctor-approved clinical notes into longitudinal health continuity.

Doctor OS Lite reduces documentation burden, works reliably under poor connectivity, and closes billing leakage through a voice-first consultation-to-billing loop.

WeCare Pocket is the patient’s health passport. It automatically receives visit summaries, prescriptions, reminders, receipts, and caregiver visibility without requiring the patient to manually enter medical data.

Product Mission

Help a doctor say within 14 days: “This software saved me time today.”
Help a patient say: “The system remembered what the clinic previously forgot.”

Positioning

  • Doctor OS Lite competes against paper, WhatsApp, memory, and abandoned EMR software — not against full hospital HIS systems.
  • WeCare Pocket competes against fragmented paper prescriptions and disorganized WhatsApp chats — not against consumer marketplaces.
  • The combined system is a healthcare coordination platform, not a pure telemedicine app.

Core Design Principles

  • Voice-first, not form-first
  • Offline-first for the core provider loop
  • Doctor approval gates all clinical content shown to patients
  • Shared source of truth across provider and consumer systems
  • Privacy by design, with auditable access and revocable sharing
  • Interoperability readiness (FHIR-aligned, optional ABHA)
  • Low training cost — if a doctor needs more than one day to feel productive, the product has failed

5. End-to-End Journeys

Journey 1 — New Patient Consultation (Phase 1)

1. Patient arrives → Front desk enters mobile number 2. System searches existing profile + duplicate heuristics 3. If no valid consent → OTP-based consent capture (includes AI disclosure) 4. Patient placed in queue 5. Doctor opens patient context screen (cached if offline) 6. Doctor starts voice capture → performs consultation 7. AI generates SOAP draft + patient_summary draft 8. Doctor reviews, edits, approves (one tap) 9. System stores immutable record + structured prescription 10. Bill auto-created and routed to front desk 11. Prescription + receipt delivered to patient via WhatsApp

Journey 2 — Returning Patient Follow-up

Doctor sees prior approved record instantly. Patient does not re-tell the full story. Updated summary, prescription, and reminders are delivered automatically after approval.

Journey 3 — Remote Caregiver (Phase 2)

Caregiver links only after explicit patient authorization. Views current medications, latest summary, next follow-up, and adherence events within granted scope. Access is revocable at any time.

Journey 4 — Offline Consultation Recovery

Core loop (context, record, draft, approve, bill event) continues on device. On reconnect, events sync in sequence. Conflicts surface for manual resolution — never silent overwrite.

6.3 Mandatory Enterprise Additions (Phase 1)

  • Doctor onboarding must verify NMC registration before note approval is enabled.
  • Prescription must be a structured entity (generic, brand, strength, frequency, duration, route, caution, signature).
  • Approved records maintain version history; corrections only via formal addendum.

  • Front-desk users never see full SOAP unless explicitly granted a clinical role.
  • Consent captured by OTP with explicit AI-processing disclosure.
  • patient_summary field generated synchronously at approval time (even if not shown to patient yet).

36–60 months · Unlocked after ≥5,000 clinics + Series B

In Scope (from original vision, correctly deferred)

  • Doctor-at-home / remote visit with GPS tracking
  • Assisted telemedicine facilitation (urban + rural)
  • Insurance claim facilitation (Indian + foreign patients)
  • Preventive screening & elder-care / caregiver marketplace expansion
  • NFC health card + offline personal QR as hardware continuity layer
  • Foreign medical tourist corridor (verified pre-booking, language, record continuity)
  • ASHA network, employer, and insurer workflows
  • Hospital / super-specialist end-to-end packages
These capabilities remain deliberately out of Phase 1–3. Introducing them earlier destroys focus, capital efficiency, and the core clinic-adoption thesis.

Critical Data Rules

  • patient_summary must be generated synchronously at doctor approval.
  • Prescription is a first-class structured entity, not only free text inside SOAP.
  • Approved clinical content is immutable; changes only via versioned addendum.
  • Phone number is the primary patient identifier across both products.

11. AI System Requirements & Guardrails

AI Responsibilities

  • Transcribe multilingual consultation speech (Hindi, Marathi first)
  • Structure speech into SOAP sections
  • Generate plain-language patient_summary
  • Surface low-confidence segments for doctor review

Hard Guardrails (Non-Negotiable)

  • AI output is draft-only until approved by a registered doctor.
  • AI never diagnoses or finalizes treatment independently.
  • Patient-facing summaries are generated only from the approved record state.
  • Audio and text processing stay inside the approved privacy boundary.

Quality Targets

  • Draft generation 15–30 seconds
  • Doctor editing burden must remain lower than pure typing
  • Explicit testing in real Indian OPD noise and accent conditions required before pilot

12. Offline-First Architecture

Mandatory Offline Scope

  • Patient context from local cache
  • Voice recording buffer
  • SOAP draft queueing or local generation fallback
  • Clinical approval event creation
  • Bill event creation for later sync
  • Queue continuity on device

Architecture Rules

  • Local encrypted store on device
  • Event queue for all create/update operations
  • Deterministic conflict-resolution policy (never silent overwrite)
  • Chunked / resumable audio upload
  • Explicit offline badge and sync-status visibility

14. Security, Identity & Access Control

  • OTP login for doctors, front desk, and patients
  • Device registration + remote session revocation
  • Rate limiting on OTP and sensitive endpoints
  • Role-based access control (clinical vs administrative)
  • Shared-device auto-logout (30 min) + re-authentication for sensitive screens
  • Encryption in transit (TLS) and at rest
  • Encrypted local storage on mobile/tablet (including audio buffers)
  • Secrets management for all third-party keys
  • Full access logs so patients can query “who saw my data”
  • Consent scope enforced at service layer, not only in UI
  • Caregiver access bounded by explicit authorization and revocation

15. Compliance Requirements

DPDP Act 2023

  • Purpose-specific consent wording + explicit AI-processing disclosure
  • Data localization (AWS ap-south-1 or equivalent India region)
  • Right to access, correction, erasure, and consent withdrawal
  • Retention policy defined per data type
  • Documented incident-response and 72-hour breach notification process

Telemedicine Practice Guidelines

  • Only a Registered Medical Practitioner may prescribe
  • AI drafts; doctor must approve and sign
  • Prescription contains doctor name + registration number, patient identifiers, diagnosis, drug names (generic mandatory)
  • Records retained 7 years

CDSCO

Doctor OS Lite expected Class A (information tool). Formal regulatory opinion recommended before first pilot.

ABDM

Optional ABHA linking only. Never forced. When linked, align with national consent manager.

Unified Recommendation

The current PRDs already contain a strong, differentiated product thesis focused on independent clinics.

The original multi-service vision (video + home visit + in-clinic + ePharmacy + labs + hospitals + insurance + NFC + foreign corridor) is correctly sequenced into later phases and must stay there.

Do not expand scope. Do not start broad engineering.

Immediately produce, in this order:

  1. Offline sync architecture
  2. Consent management specification
  3. AI model card + guardrails
  4. Database ERD + schema dictionary
  5. OpenAPI contracts for the core loop

Only after these five documents are reviewed and signed off should sprint planning and implementation begin.

WeCare LIFES is a healthcare coordination platform for small-clinic India. Its success depends on operational completeness, not additional features.
